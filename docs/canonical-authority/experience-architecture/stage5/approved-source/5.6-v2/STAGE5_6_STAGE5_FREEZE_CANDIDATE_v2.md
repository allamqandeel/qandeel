## 7. Event-vs-action register

| Class | Members | `RH` authority |
|-------|---------|----------------|
| **Explicit user actions** | The sixteen in §6 | May create **one** transaction **iff** the act produces an **action-attributable** change to `Φ_eff` |
| **Passive events** | new committed Live content advancing `LH`; `LF` change; then-current Live analytical evolution; derived `K(TC)` / `IF_render` re-resolution; Preview pointer movement | **Never** — under any circumstance |

**Proof that concurrency confers no authority.** Transaction authority is a predicate of an *act*: `writes_RH(a) ⇔ effective_attributable(a)`, where attribution is by causation, not by coincidence in time. A passive event `p` concurrent with an act `a` contributes `Δ(p)` to `S`; since `p ∉ actions`, `Δ(p)` is by definition not attributable to `a`, so it cannot change `effective_attributable(a)`. The decisive worked case is **AT-25**: under `FOLLOW_LIVE`, `TC` tracks `LH`, so a passive advance *does* change a `Φ_eff` component while an invoked Return to Live Head changes nothing attributable to it — the command remains a no-op and writes nothing, while `TC` advances passively. Without the attribution qualifier the naive `Φ_eff` test would wrongly report a transaction here. **Z-04: PASS.**

## 33. AMB-01 — **RESOLVED BY ARCHITECTURE RULING**

**Status: RESOLVED.** Architecture confirmed AMB-01 as a genuine ambiguity (not a contradiction) and resolved it by a narrow Stage 5.5 reopening: **P5 uses post-live one-shot binding.** The original v1 finding is retained below **as evidence**, per the ruling's instruction — it is the record that the higher-order audit worked, that the gap was escalated rather than absorbed, and that no rule was invented to paper over it.

**Resolution as incorporated.** P5 is one atomic composite with ordered sub-effects: (1) the temporal sub-effect establishes `FOLLOW_LIVE` and resolves effective `TC` to the authoritative Live Head; (2) at that **post-live boundary** the act snapshots the then-current Live Focus exactly once as `P5_LF*`; (3) at locate settle the spatial sub-effect executes **iff** `Locatable(P5_LF*, K(LH))`. Later `LF` changes never retarget an in-flight P5, and no persistent follow is created. `P5_LF*` is transient and act-local — **not** a member of `S`. No intermediate `RH` checkpoint, Back stop, temporal mode or state member arises; if the temporal part changes state but the locate cannot execute, the transaction still records the effective composite act under the existing frozen `RH` rules, and Back restores the full pre-P5 checkpoint via `RestoreTemporal(C_RH) := PINNED(C_RH.capturedTC)`.

**Required race outcomes, both now determinate.** *Case A* — `LF` moves Established `A` → Emerging `B` before the boundary: `P5_LF* = B`, `Locatable(B, K(LH)) = false`, so `FOLLOW_LIVE` with `TC = LH` and **no camera movement**; `A` is **not** located merely for having been `LF` at activation. *Case B* — `LF` changes *after* binding: the act does not chase; at settle it locates `A` once if still legitimate, otherwise moves nothing. A later explicit P5 may bind the then-current `LF`.

**Why this is the principled boundary, and why D1 is untouched.** The composite's meaning is sequential: its first effect deliberately changes the temporal frame to Live, so its spatial target must be selected from Live state *after* that return has taken effect — binding earlier could send the camera to something that is no longer the Live Focus at the moment "Go Live" is actually achieved. Continuous chasing to final settle is equally rejected, because it would let asynchronous `LF` changes retarget one explicit command repeatedly and blur a one-shot composite into a follow. D1 therefore remains exactly as frozen for **Return to Live Focus** — a spatial-only act operating in the user's *current* temporal frame, bound at activation — and the two actions bind at different semantic boundaries for a stated reason rather than by exception. Neither ever follows later `LF` changes, which is the invariant both rules exist to protect (I-18).

**Nothing else moved.** The ruling changes no other Stage 5.5 rule and does not touch no-hindsight, historical projection, Thread Home loci, Emerging semantics, Stage 4 locate authority, Stage 5.3 disclosure or accessibility parity. The spatial sub-effect stays bounded by existing Stage 4 / P5 locate authority, and no fabricated locus is permitted.

**3 — The missing semantic decision.**
The binding time of P5's locate referent: activation-captured `LF*`, or settle-time `LF`.

**4 — Ambiguity or contradiction.**
**Ambiguity.** Both readings satisfy every applicable frozen rule; the intersection is non-empty but contains two semantically different canonical outcomes (camera moves to `A` / camera does not move). Under FG-01 this affects **camera authority** and **return behaviour** and therefore blocks freeze. It is **not** a contradiction: no frozen rule is violated by either reading.

**5 — Smallest upstream reopening.**
A **single clause added to Stage 5.5**, scoped to P5 alone. Two candidate clarifications were offered without preference:
- **(a) Activation-binding** — `LF*` captured at activation, mirroring D1; consequence: the camera moves to `A`.
- **(b) Settle-binding** — the guard evaluates the `LF` current at settle; consequence: no camera movement.

No other stage required reopening, and nothing else in Stage 5 depended on the choice: 49 of 50 acceptance tests and 19 of 20 integrated attacks were unaffected either way.

**Secondary observation offered to Architecture (not a second blocker).** Under (a), P5 and Return to Live Focus bind identically; under (b) they diverge, so two nearly identical user-facing acts would resolve their referent differently.

**How Architecture ruled.** Neither candidate exactly: a **third, narrower boundary** — bind once at the *post-live* handoff between the composite's temporal and spatial sub-effects. This rejects (a) because the spatial target must be chosen from the Live frame the command itself establishes, and rejects continuous chasing because that would turn a one-shot act into a follow. In the mandated race it yields (b)'s outcome — no camera movement — but for a sequential reason rather than a settle-time one, and it preserves D1 unchanged. The secondary observation is answered directly: the two acts bind at different boundaries **by design**, because one changes the temporal frame first and the other does not.
