# C3_EXPRESSIVE_HEADROOM_CONTRACT

**I-08B3.1-C3.5.** Mandatory.

**Purpose: to prevent a future implementer from reading the frozen neutral system as a mandate for
visual austerity.**

---

## 1. Why this document is necessary

A specification stage is dangerous in one specific way. Almost everything it writes is a prohibition,
because prohibitions are what can be checked. By the time C3 closes, the C track will have produced:

- one colour, used sparingly;
- a list of places it may not go, longer than the list of places it may;
- twenty-one machine-testable rules, nineteen of which are negative;
- five adversarial tests, all of which ask what must not be true.

Read in isolation, that reads as an instruction to keep QANDEEL quiet. **It is not.** It is a set of
constraints on ONE pillar of the identity, written at the moment that pillar was being frozen, by a
stage that was forbidden from touching any of the others.

The absence of the other pillars from this package is a **scope boundary, not a verdict.**

## 3. C3 MUST NOT RESERVE ALL VISUAL SALIENCE FOR BRASS

**Brass is one identity pillar. It is not the personality of QANDEEL.**

Nothing in the material contract claims a monopoly on being noticed. In particular, a future system is
**not** required to be quieter than Living Brass, dimmer than Living Brass, or subordinate to Living
Brass. Those would all be reasonable-sounding readings of "restraint" and none of them follows from
anything C3 froze.

### 3.1 What C3 froze about navigation, stated exactly

C3 freezes **the Living Brass material permission** of the persistent navigation icon family.

C3 explicitly does **NOT** freeze:

- final tab-bar geometry
- the tab container form
- the final selected-state treatment
- label behaviour
- motion or transition
- the active indicator
- tab depth
- final icon geometry, beyond already-canonical brand assets

**A future tab system must be free to become visually distinctive.** C3 must not have reduced it to
*"gold icons on a black bar"*, and it has not: it has said what the icons are made of and nothing
else.

## 4. The lantern, and the authority boundaries around it

Existing Product direction includes an exceptional authentication / onboarding identity moment: a
physical lantern can appear at the gateway, the user interacts with it, and light reveals or activates
the Q identity.

**C3 does not design it, does not freeze its animation, and does not make the lantern a mascot or a
recurring default object.** It records the boundaries so the systems can compose later without one of
them having quietly annexed another's territory:

| System | Owns |
|---|---|
| **LANTERN** | an exceptional gateway identity object |
| **LIVING BRASS** | material identity |
| **QANDEEL LIGHT** | later illumination and meaning |
| **MOTION** | interaction and continuity |

The lantern moment is precisely where several of these must meet. Nothing in C3 obstructs that: the
material contract grants material, the illumination namespace is reserved and empty, and no motion
rule exists to conflict with.

## 5. The measured reason this contract is not theoretical

`C3_VISUAL_VITALITY_DIRECTIVE.md` §4 runs the Life Test on the accepted compositions and returns
**INCOMPLETE**. Roughly 93–97.5 % of QANDEEL's ink is achromatic; the material occupies 2.51–6.64 % of
ink depending on the environment.

**That is not a deficiency in the material.** It is the measurement of how much of the Product's
visual identity is still owed by systems that do not exist yet. This contract is the record that they
are owed, and that they are permitted.

---

## 6. The question a future stage should ask

Not:

> *"Can this be removed?"*

But:

> **"Is this expressive element truthful and authored?"**

Removal tests exist to find unnecessary UI. They were never a method for deciding how much identity a
product should have, and using them that way produces a product that passes every test and that
nobody wants to open.

See `C3_ANTI_MINIMALISM_MISREAD.md` for the specific frozen rules most likely to be quoted in the
wrong direction, and the correction for each.
