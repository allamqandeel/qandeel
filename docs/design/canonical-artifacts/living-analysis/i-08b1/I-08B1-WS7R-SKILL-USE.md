## WS7R-M2 (the session-frame morphology correction) — additional record

The brief for this pass did **not** mandate a skill re-read, and none was performed. What is
recorded here is the already-loaded discipline that actually shaped the work, so the record stays
honest about which passes were skill-driven and which were not.

### `frontend-design` — the self-critique step, and it earned its keep
The first cut of `ringFacet` ran the existing ring's own thresholds and drew **88% of the
perimeter at one clamped alpha**. Rendered, that is a hard even polygon — which is both the
generic answer to "make it faceted" and the exact thing the brief rules out ("Do NOT turn the
session frames into hard UI polygons"). It was rejected on the render, not on the plan, and the
rebuild is what produced the three weights, the 35.5%-absent perimeter, the angular vertex
displacement and the un-clamped `alf`. Without a deliberate look-at-it-and-reject step this pass
would have shipped the first cut, because the first cut technically satisfied every word of the
request.

### `emil-design-eng` — twice, in the same pass
- ***"When unsure, the strongest move is often to delete it."*** The correction is mostly
  deletion: 35.5% of each perimeter absent, the circular halo cut 28%, the smooth ring skipped
  entirely once `S.map` reaches 1. What was added — 48 strokes and 86 node sprites — is paid for
  out of those.
- **A clamped value is a deleted signal.** The diagnosis that the ring's bright weight was running
  at α 1.44 and being clamped to 1 is the reason three weights and a presence field were
  invisible. Worth generalising: *before tuning a variation, check that the variable is not
  saturated.*
- ***Build a switch.*** No new switch was added — `MAPON` already gates this distance, and the
  whole pass rides `S.map`. One boolean still turns all of it off.

### `designing-arabic-frontends` — the check that stopped it going further
The obvious next move after lighting the vertices was to make the nodes bigger and brighter until
the envelope really sang. **"Never read Arabic through light"** is what stopped it: `جلسة ١١`,
`جلسة ١٣` and `مشروع جانبي` sit close enough to their own perimeters that a larger node would sit
under a name. Node radius is capped at 7.2% of `s.r` for that reason, and `__typeAudit()` is
re-verified on the delivered file — letter-spacing 0px, direction rtl, weight in the Arabic range,
IBM Plex Sans Arabic loaded.

### Not applied, deliberately
1. **Faceting the territory rims.** Out of scope by the brief's own limits, and it turns out to be
   better left alone: the parent now reads as a soft circular region and the child as a faceted
   envelope, so containment is carried by a difference in *kind*. Notes §18.7.1.
2. **`review-animations`.** Still gated to explicit user invocation on this host, and the brief
   did not ask for it. §18.6 measures the ramp instead and reports it got *gentler*
   (2.433% → 2.304% steepest relative step); §17.8.1's standing request is unchanged.
3. **Raising node count or brightness with session size or activity.** Vertex count follows
   circumference only. Anything else would be a semantic this pass has no authority to invent.

## WS7R-M (the MID world-territory map) — additional record

Re-read and applied before the MID composition was written, per the brief.

### `frontend-design`
- **"The hero is a thesis"** set the whole shape of the pass. The first move was not tuning
  WS7's MID but naming what MID is *for* — "what does my world look like?" against NEAR's
  "what is QANDEEL working on here?" — and the thesis that fell out of that is §17.1: MID has
  the same substrate inversion WS7R fixed at NEAR, so this is the pass the schedule always
  implied and not a new aesthetic. Everything else in the pass is downstream of that sentence.
- **"Structural devices should encode something true, not decorate."** This is why the MID
  structure board uses a **colour key** and not numbered markers `01 / 02 / 03`. The five things
  it points at are not a sequence, and numbering them would assert an order the map does not
  have. It also rejected an earlier version of that board whose callout labels collided with the
  map's own Arabic — legible beats clever.
- **Self-critique against the generic default** rejected the first territory fill. A bright
  filled disc is what any tool produces for "make the region visible", and set beside the
  reference at full size it was plainly wrong: the reference's interiors are nearly black and
  all of its light is in the perimeter and the particles. The fill dropped to three fifths and
  the rim took the work (§17.3).

### `emil-design-eng`
- **"When unsure, the strongest move is often to delete it."** Generalised from motion to
  material for the second time in this workshop. The MID pass adds particles, fills and rims,
  and it pays for all of them by deletion: cores withdrawn, census cut, currents widened and
  dimmed, outward atmosphere −68%, cosmos −34%. Net draw calls **fall**, 11,687 → 11,494.
- **"Build a switch for the thing you are tuning."** `MAPON`, matching `LODON` / `K` / `J` /
  `W` / `S` / `D`. Every measurement in §17 is a toggle of one boolean on one build rather than
  a comparison of two builds that might have drifted.
- **"Remove one accessory."** Cut the per-callout leader lines and prose from the structure
  board once the colour key carried the same information.

### `designing-arabic-frontends`
- **"Never read Arabic through light."** The reason the territory fill was measured *under the
  labels* and not only globally: `العمل`'s name sits over its own bed, and a fill bright enough
  to define a region is a fill bright enough to eat a label. The shipped bed is 0.205 α at 23%
  lightness, and `__typeAudit()` is re-verified on the delivered file — letter-spacing 0px,
  direction rtl, weights 400–700, IBM Plex Sans Arabic loaded.
- **Label *tiers* must stay distinguishable.** MID now shows territory names and session names
  at once over far more material than WS7's MID did. The census (`labTer`, `labSes`, `labObj`
  = 1 / 1 / 0 at MID) was left exactly as approved rather than raising object labels to match
  the new density — eight names at a readable weight, not sixteen at 13%.
- It is also the reason **no status line was invented** (§17.6.2). The reference's
  `(نشط)` / `(خامل)` are a second Arabic tier that would have to be right in register, weight
  and leading — and the deeper problem is that neither state exists in canonical data.

### Not applied, deliberately
1. **The reference's inter-territory web.** Its signature is a bright particulate web filling
   the space between territories. Not built: the brief itself diverges from the image here
   (*"Between territories: only canonical / authorized relationships"*), and it would be the
   global mesh under better lighting.
2. **The reference's ~15 territories and its status lines and anchor glyphs.** §17.6. The
   decisive reason for the territories is not taste — `TER` feeds the world build's random
   stream, so one more territory rebuilds all 233 objects and breaks the frozen NEAR baseline.
3. **The WS7R §8 MID density proposal (`K`).** Still off. It is a different, unreviewed change
   to the same distance; mixing it into a commissioned pass would make neither legible.
4. **`review-animations`.** Not invoked — the brief says not yet, and on this host it is gated
   to explicit user invocation anyway. §17.8.1 records the one motion-adjacent cost and asks
   for it to be run by hand before MID is frozen.

## Applied, before the composition

### `frontend-design`
Used exactly as its own process asks — plan, self-critique the plan against the generic
default, *then* build. Three things it changed:

- **It set the order of work.** Its "the hero is a thesis" framing is what made the first
  move a composition decision (*what is NEAR* for*) rather than another pass over the
  relation alpha equations, which is what the brief warned against in its first paragraph.
- **The signature element.** Its instruction to spend boldness in one place is why WS7R has
  one memorable move — the session as a luminous irregular place (§3.1) — rather than five
  smaller improvements. The self-critique step is what rejected the first idea for it: a new
  blurred sprite is the generic answer to "make the focus feel like somewhere". Every layer
  of the shipped version reuses a mechanism the file already had (the rim's own noise
  wobble, the existing focus crossfade, the existing subtractive ground).
- **The risk, named and justified.** Its "not taking a risk is a risk" note is why the pass
  deletes the substrate population at NEAR (`cut` 1.00 → 0.12) instead of dimming it. The
  justification is in §2; the switch to judge it against is `L`.

### `emil-design-eng`
- ***"When unsure, the strongest move is often to delete it."*** Generalised from motion to
  composition, this is the whole of §2. WS6 and WS7 both answered density by changing how
  8,180 strokes *look*; WS7R changes how many are *drawn*. A hundred faint traces are still
  a hundred traces.
- ***Build a switch for the thing you are tuning.*** Three of them: `L` for the resolution
  change, `K` for the MID proposal, `J` for the FAR proposal — matching `W`, `S` and `D`.
  `L` is also what makes `ws7r-compare-near.png` a switch rather than two builds that might
  have drifted apart.
- ***"Remove one accessory."*** Cut a shrink-on-voice term for low-voice object marks in the
  first draft, on the grounds that the glyph-integration change already did the work without
  raising the "size encodes rank" question. The render then showed the marks needed it after
  all, so it came back — at a higher floor (0.58 rather than 0.42) and flagged in the notes
  as limitation 1. Removing it first is why it is a judged, bounded term and not an
  unexamined one.

### `designing-arabic-frontends`
Three concrete changes, all from re-reading it against this frame:

- **Arabic gets more room, not less.** Its "Arabic needs ≥1.6 leading and never
  letter-spacing" discipline is the reason the label correction is a **census** change
  rather than an opacity change: the fix for eight competing names is fewer names at a
  readable weight, not eight names at 13%. Constellation names are now ~12% larger at NEAR.
- **Never read Arabic through light.** It is what made the focus bloom's 500px radius a
  defect rather than a style: the focus's own Arabic label sat inside it. The bloom halves
  on approach (§3.2).
- **`lang` drives font fallback and the screen-reader voice, and `dir` alone does not.**
  Re-verified on the delivered file: the HUD world-name row still carries `lang="ar"
  dir="rtl"`, and `__typeAudit()` reports `letterSpacing 0px`, `direction rtl`, weight in
  400–700, IBM Plex Sans Arabic loaded, `#evlog` at 1.7 leading.

### `animate-expo`
Used as the mobile-reality constraint on what this pass was allowed to add.

- ***"Reduced motion ships with the animation, not after it."*** Checked before writing
  anything: the resolution change is a **static** schedule term — a function of the camera,
  not of time — so it has no reduced-motion path to drift, and the brief's reduced-motion
  requirement is satisfied by construction rather than by a second code path (§11).
- ***"`transform` and `opacity` are free; everything else is a layout pass."*** The canvas
  analogue is the draw call, and it is why the pass was steered toward terms that **remove**
  draws (a quantile cut) over terms that **add** them (a new stratum). §10 is the result:
  NEAR at 926 strokes against WS7's 7,586.

### `react-native-best-practices` → `references/animations`, `canvas-atlas.md`
The portability constraint on where the new work may run.

- A scene of this size on device is **`Atlas` + `useTexture` + `useRSXformBuffer`**: one
  draw call, per-instance RSX transforms, a worklet that must be O(1) per instance. That is
  what a **quantile** census buys and an alpha ramp does not — `f.q > cut` is an instance
  filter a worklet can evaluate, while "everything, fainter" is 8,180 instances however
  faint they are.
- Two new per-frame costs were checked against it and both are O(1): `ringFill` is cached in
  the existing `WOB` path map and built once; the session field allocates one gradient per
  frame and only for a session actually mid-transfer.
- **Not solved and stated as such:** §10's closing paragraph. A release build on the slowest
  supported device is still the only thing that counts as verification, and this host cannot
  produce it.

## Recommendations deliberately NOT applied

**1. The constellation reference's semantic encodings.** `constellation-map-spec.md` ties
particle count, glow strength, circle size and position to activity and importance. Its
*material* was used throughout — deep black base, broad low-opacity coloured fields, the
three-layer territory build, organic overlap between neighbouring regions, curved connective
lines, colour identity. Its *encodings* were not imported, because none of them is
authorised by canonical runtime state and T-10 governs. Territory hue remains an identity,
not a rank.

**2. The reference's infinite pulsing.** §1's background stars carry a 3–6 s opacity loop.
Not applied: decorative looping motion exceeds T-10 and is exactly what the `X` quarantine
exists to keep out of the compliant frame.

**3. `frontend-design` on the harness chrome.** Still out of scope, for the same reason WS7
gave: the chrome is not the deliverable and changing it would make the WS7 comparison boards
inconsistent for no gain to the question being asked. The three new controls inherit
`.tbtn` — hit-slop, `:active` scale, `@media (hover: hover)` gating — rather than
re-implementing any of it.

**4. `emil-design-eng` / "never animate keyboard-initiated actions".** The `1`–`5` event
keys and `F`/`M`/`N` still animate. Unchanged from WS7 and for its reason: in this prototype
a key is not a UI shortcut, it is the simulator for a runtime cause, and the reaction is the
subject of the study.

**5. Applying the MID and FAR findings.** Both are measured, both are implemented, both ship
**off**. `emil-design-eng`'s bias toward deletion and `frontend-design`'s toward a decisive
point of view would both have argued for turning them on. Not applied: they are changes to
compositions Product has already reviewed, the brief says in as many words not to casually
redesign MID, and a proposal Product can toggle is worth more than a change Product has to
undo. See notes §8, §9 and limitation 4.

## WS7R-F — the FAR reconstruction (2026-09-20)

Skills actively used, as the brief required: **frontend-design**, **emil-design-eng**,
**impeccable**. `designing-arabic-frontends` was consulted and found **not applicable to this
pass**, because §F4's whole result is that there is no Arabic — or any other type — in the FAR
frame at all. Its rules still govern the type that arrives from A = 0.055 onward, and that type
is the frozen MID stratum, untouched.

**1. `frontend-design`: the design plan before the code.** The plan was written first and
reviewed against the brief before a line changed, in the skill's four terms.
- *Colour*: **no new palette.** This is the one place where the skill's "distinctive point of
  view" pressure had to be refused outright — FAR is a distant projection of a frozen visual
  system, so inventing a palette would be the failure, not the achievement. The six identity
  hues and the cosmos's 186–232 are carried verbatim.
- *Type*: **none**, and that is the type decision.
- *Layout*: the world's own hull, unmoved.
- *Signature*: **the terminator.** The skill's instruction to spend boldness in exactly one
  place, and the one element the frame is meant to be remembered by. It earns its place three
  times over: it makes the world a body, it binds the subject to its nineteen background worlds
  through the shared `LIGHT_DIR`, and it produces the "darker side / deeper regions" the brief
  asks for without drawing anything.

**2. `frontend-design`'s calibration warning, inverted usefully.** The skill warns that
AI-generated design clusters around three defaults. The relevant analogue here is the
"near-black background with a single bright accent" cluster, and the honest reading is that a
cosmic scene is *intrinsically* that cluster. What kept it from being a default was that every
choice was forced by the frozen material: the hues are MID's, the light direction is WS6's, the
shape is WS5's hull, the density field is §R1's. The pass took one real risk — removing every
name from the frame a reader sees first — and the brief is what justifies it.

**3. `emil-design-eng` / the remedial hierarchy: delete first.** Applied literally and it is
most of §F1. The strongest moves in this pass are subtractions: four drawn containers withdrawn,
every label removed, the focus ring removed, the filament census cut by three quarters. The
additive instruments (§F3's three passes, the aerosol, the nebular pass) exist only to carry
what the subtractions took away. Measured, the pass is net-negative in draw calls at A = 0
(11,391 against 11,435), which is the honest test of "delete before you add".

**4. `impeccable`: bounded verification, not an open loop.** The skill's rule is build fully,
inspect once in a batched round, fix everything that round shows, confirm with at most one more
— and the brief says the same thing in its own words ("ONE strong FAR reconstruction... avoid
multiple micro-patch rounds"). Followed with one deliberate exception: the first render showed
six perfect circles and the second showed concentric contour arcs on the limb. Both are
*defects*, not preferences — the brief names "UI circle" and "technical outline" as
self-rejection criteria — so they were fixed and re-rendered rather than shipped and reported.
Balance stopped there. No taste-level polish round was run.

**5. Where the skills would have gone further and were held back.** `impeccable`'s
"dream big and bold" and `frontend-design`'s "take one real aesthetic risk" both argued for
giving the active world a stronger, more literal planetary silhouette — a defined terminator
across a felt sphere. Not applied: the brief rules out a perfect circle and a technical outline,
and the mandatory continuity requirement means the FAR body must be the MID hull, which is a
lobed organic coastline and not a sphere. The result is a weaker body read than the skills would
want, and it is recorded as remaining weakness 2 and 3 in notes §26.8 rather than argued away.

**6. What the pass record caught that no skill would have.** The 150-degrees-apart hue trap
(notes §26.5). `frontend-design` would have approved "pull the hues toward the cosmic blue for
aerial perspective" as a sound, even sophisticated, colour decision. It turned the world green,
and the only reason it was caught in one render rather than shipped is that this workshop has
been carrying the rule since §W5.
