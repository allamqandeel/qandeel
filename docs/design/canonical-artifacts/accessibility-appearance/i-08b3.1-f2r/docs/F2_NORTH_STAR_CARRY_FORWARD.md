# I-08B3.1-F2 — NORTH STAR / SPECTACLE CARRY-FORWARD

## The status, in I-08B3.1-F1's own words, unchanged

> **NORTH STAR SPECTACLE = OPEN. NOT PROVEN BY F1, NOT WEAKENED BY F1, OWNED BY G.**

§25 requires F2 to preserve that wording. **It is preserved exactly, and F2 adds nothing to it.**

F2 may gather useful evidence. **It must not declare the final North Star requirement closed, and it
does not.** Check **G-01** scans every document and the README for a sentence claiming the North Star
was achieved, met, reached, satisfied, closed or proven, and for the presence of the OPEN status and
G's ownership. Its probe is the sentence *"the North Star was achieved by this package"*.

## What F2 did NOT do with the North Star image

The supplied image was created in a dark visual environment. §11 says: **do not literally recreate
its dark-space aesthetic in Light.** F2 does not. It does not recreate it in dark either — the
Living Analysis World in this package is I-08B3.1-D2R's, and the distance between D2R's world and
the North Star is exactly the distance F1 measured and G owns.

**F2 ran no spectacle measurement and makes no spectacle claim.** I-08B3.1-F1 measured painted-pixel
and ceiling ratios against the North Star and reported them; F2 does not repeat that, because
repeating it in a second appearance would invite the reading that the light appearance had been
judged against the North Star and passed.

## The obligation F2 DOES carry: not making G's job impossible

> **F2 MUST NOT introduce Light-Appearance constraints that make the required G spectacle
> impossible. Record any such risk.** — §25

Four risks are recorded. **None is judged to close the door, and the fourth is the one worth
arguing about.**

### RISK 1 — The luminance range is spent, and it cannot be spent twice

The dark appearance's expressive headroom comes from a near-empty ground: everything from L 0.17 to
L 0.98 is available for the world's atmosphere, its meaning events and its identity material. **The
light appearance has already spent the top of that range on the ground itself.**

Measured: a meaning event's area mean across its own source disc is **dEok 0.2846** in dark and
**0.1022** in light; its per-pixel peak is **0.6925** against **0.1570**. In each appearance's own
smallest reading steps — the only unit in which the two mean the same thing — **2.483 against 1.005**.

**Why it does not close the door.** Spectacle on a light ground is not made of luminance — it is
made of **density, contour, layering, colour relation and scale**, all of which the light appearance
has in the same quantity as the dark one, because they are the same field. But **a G proof that
relies on luminous range will not transfer**, and G should design its spectacle proof knowing that.

### RISK 2 — The Light World's lightness is bounded from above by the meaning event

`qandeel.appearance.light-world-lightness` = 0.95 is the **highest** value at which a source still
rises. If G's spectacle wants a brighter, more open light ground, **it is in direct competition with
the meaning event's visibility**, and one of the two has to give.

**Recorded rather than resolved.** The trade is visible in the sweep in `data/F2_DERIVATION.json`:
every candidate ground from 0.880 to 0.985, with what each one leaves the source.

### RISK 3 — The chroma ceiling is derived from the Light, and the Light is quieter in light

D2R derives the atmosphere's chroma ceiling as `0.62 × the least chromatic Light stop`. The light
appearance's Light carries slightly less chroma, so **the light appearance's atmosphere is
permitted slightly less colour than the dark one's**: 0.0193 against 0.0197.

**It is a 2% difference and it is not the constraint it looks like.** But if G's spectacle wants a
more chromatic ambient field, the derivation says it must first make the Light more chromatic — and
that in turn pushes against Living Brass, which must stay above it. **The ladder is the constraint,
not the number**, and it is the same ladder in both appearances.

### RISK 4 — The glaze is the thing that carries the light event, and it is deliberately small

The selection rule took **the mildest extreme the floor forces**, after two earlier rules ran to a
bound and produced a wash over half the world. That is the right rule for a Product surface.

**It may be the wrong rule for a spectacle moment.** A once-in-a-session arrival, at world scale,
with the camera on it, may want a larger event than the floor requires — and the parameters that
would give it are searched, recorded and classified **production-default**: `bloom.split` and
`bloom.glaze-width`.

> **G MAY MOVE THEM. F2 RECORDS THAT THEY ARE MOVABLE, AND THAT THE CEILING ON MOVING THEM IS
> R-FOOTPRINT, R-INK-SEPARATION AND R-SOURCE-DOMINANCE RATHER THAN TASTE.** What G may not do is
> change the technique's contract — a meaning event still has a source, still settles to exactly
> nothing, still stays separable from the identity material, and still survives having its colour
> removed.

**AND G SHOULD KNOW HOW MUCH ROOM IS ACTUALLY THERE, BECAUSE I-08B3.1-F2R MEASURED IT.** On this
ground the room is small. Making the light event stand to its own reading ramp as the dark event
stands to its own is **reachable and inadmissible**: every one of the 2,379 configurations that get
there paints a stop darker than the World under its own passage scrim, and all 2,379 fail that one
requirement and no other. A spectacle moment that wants more luminous magnitude in the light
appearance needs **a different ground**, not a different bloom — and lowering the Light World is the
lever, with its cost recorded in the sweep. G is free to make that trade; F2 is not, because the
ground is what the analysis is drawn on.
